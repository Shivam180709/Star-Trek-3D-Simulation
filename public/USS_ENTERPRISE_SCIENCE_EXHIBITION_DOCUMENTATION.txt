1.1 WHAT IS THIS PROJECT?
-------------------------
The USS Enterprise (NCC-1701) 3D Simulation is a real-time, interactive 3D
spaceflight, bridge-command, and astrophysical laboratory web application running
directly in the browser at 60 frames per second (FPS).

Unlike static planetarium viewers or pre-rendered videos, this software builds an
entire living galaxy in three-dimensional space using procedural geometry,
physically based rendering (PBR), custom GPU shaders, and real NASA/planetary
maps. Users step into the role of Captain and Bridge Crew of the legendary
Constitution-class starship USS Enterprise (NCC-1701). From either an external
3D Chase/Orbit camera or an interactive First-Person 3D Bridge Interior, users
can:
  * Pilot the Enterprise across 30 canonical Star Systems spanning the Alpha,
    Beta, Gamma, and Delta Quadrants.
  * Experience realistic Astronomical Unit (AU) planetary distances and
    mathematically synchronized velocities in kilometers per second (km/s) and
    multiples of the speed of light (c).
  * Switch seamlessly between 5 Bridge Crew Stations (Captain's Chair, Helm &
    Navigation, Tactical & Weapons, Science & Astrometrics, Communications &
    Engineering) inside a 3D multi-tiered bridge arena.
  * Conduct real-world astrophysical experiments—including Fraunhofer/Balmer
    atomic spectroscopy, magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) planetary core restoration,
    and radio-astronomy First Contact frequency calibration.
  * Engage in tactical starship defense against hostile Klingon, Romulan,
    Cardassian, Gorn, Tholian, and Borg vessels, then dock at monumental
    Deep-Space Spacedocks for automated drone hull repairs and antimatter
    refueling.

1.2 WHY STAR TREK IN A SCIENCE EXHIBITION? (SCI-FI MEETS REAL ASTROPHYSICS)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Since its debut in 1966, Star Trek has inspired generations of NASA engineers,
astrophysicists, and computer scientists—from the invention of the flip phone
and tablet computer to real theoretical physics research such as the Alcubierre
Warp Metric (1994).

In a science exhibition setting, abstract physics equations (like the Rydberg
formula for hydrogen emission lines, the Lorentz force in planetary magnetic
fields, or relativistic Schwarzschild event horizons) can feel intimidating on a
poster board. This simulation transforms those exact equations into hands-on,
interactive starship instruments:
  1. KINEMATICS & ASTRONOMICAL SCALES: Visitors immediately grasp the difference
     between speed (multiples of light speed 'c' and 'km/s') and astronomical
     distance ('AU' and 'Light-Years') by watching the ship's real-time velocity
     vector directly drive the rate of change of distance (Delta-d / Delta-t) on
     the Tactical Radar.
  2. QUANTUM SPECTROSCOPY: Visitors tune a virtual grating spectrometer to match
     real Balmer H-Alpha (656.28 nm), Doubly Ionized Oxygen [O III] (500.7 nm),
     Ionized Helium He-II (468.6 nm), and Iron K-Shell X-Ray (185.0 nm) lines.
  3. MAGNETOHYDRODYNAMICS (MHD): Visitors calculate cyclotron resonance and
     Lorentz force deflection to save a planet's atmosphere from a Coronal Mass
     Ejection (CME) or Geodynamo collapse—learning why Earth has auroras and why
     Mars lost its ancient atmosphere.
  4. RADIO ASTRONOMY & GAME THEORY: Visitors align subspace transceivers to the
     real Neutral Hydrogen 21-cm hyperfine line (1420.4 MHz) and apply Nash
     Bargaining and Stag-Hunt game theory during First Contact diplomacy.

2.4 REAL-TIME REACT <-> THREE.JS STATE BRIDGE (60 FPS ARCHITECTURE)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
A common pitfall in React + Three.js apps is triggering React re-renders 60 times
per second on every `requestAnimationFrame` tick, which causes UI lag. This
application solves that with a decoupled two-tier architecture:
  1. The Three.js `SimulationEngine` (`src/three/simulationEngine.ts`) runs an
     uninterrupted `requestAnimationFrame` loop at 60+ FPS, updating 3D physics,
     quaternions, particle systems, phaser beams, torpedo trajectories, and
     bridge canvas monitors directly on the WebGL canvas.
  2. Telemetry snapshots (`ShipState`) are broadcast to React via a throttled
     callback (`broadcastState`), while heavy astrometric distance lists update
     on a 600ms interval. This keeps the 3D viewport silky-smooth while React
     HUD numbers remain responsive and accurate.

3.1 ASTRONOMICAL DISTANCE SCALE CALIBRATION (AU, KM, SCENE UNITS)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
To render an entire solar system from the Sun out to Pluto and a distant Black
Hole in WebGL without floating-point z-fighting while displaying real-world
astronomical distances, the simulation uses a calibrated linear scale defined in
`/src/utils/scaleFormatters.ts`:

  * 1 Astronomical Unit (AU) = 149,597,870.7 km (exact IAU definition: the mean
    distance between Earth and the Sun).
  * In the 3D scene coordinate space:
      1 AU = 235 Scene Units
      1 Scene Unit = 149,597,870.7 km / 235 = 636,586.6838 km
  * Example Sol System Calibration:
      - Earth Orbit (`[0, 0, -1450]`): ~6.17 AU from Sol Center
      - Saturn Orbit (`[3700, -35, 3600]`): ~21.97 AU
      - Pluto & Charon (`[7400, 65, 5600]`): ~39.49 AU (matches Pluto's real
        orbital range of 30 to 49 AU!)
      - Sol Singularity Black Hole (`[11335, 110, 8600]`): ~60.5 AU (situated
        21 AU beyond Pluto's orbit so its gravity never disturbs planetary orbits).

3.2 SPEED (c AND km/s) VS. DISTANCE (AU AND LIGHT-YEARS)
--------------------------------------------------------
A critical physics concept highlighted in this project is the rigorous
distinction between units of SPEED and units of DISTANCE:
  * "c" (Speed of Light) is a UNIT OF SPEED:
      1 c = 299,792.458 km/s (approx. 3.00 x 10^5 km/s)
      In Astronomical Units per second:
      1 c = 299,792.458 / 149,597,870.7 ≈ 0.002004 AU/s (1 AU every ~499 seconds,
      which is why sunlight takes 8 minutes 19 seconds to reach Earth).
  * "Light-Year" (LY) is a UNIT OF DISTANCE (the distance light travels in one
    Julian year of 31,557,600 seconds):
      1 LY = 9,460,730,472,580.8 km ≈ 63,241.077 AU
  * Therefore, traveling at "8.5c" (8.5 times the speed of light = 2,548,236 km/s)
    is a speed, whereas "8.5 Light-Years" is an interstellar distance of over
    80 trillion kilometers. The simulation's telemetry strictly displays speed in
    `km/s`, `AU/s`, and `c`, and displays distances in `AU` (and `LY` for
    multi-quadrant encyclopedia references).

3.3 EXACT KINEMATIC SYNCHRONIZATION (WHY DELTA-D / DELTA-T = VELOCITY)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
To ensure that a judge or student watching the Chase Flight Telemetry menu and
the Tactical Radar sees 100% mathematical consistency:
  1. At every frame, the ship's 3D velocity vector `this.velocity` has a
     magnitude `V_scene` in Scene Units per second.
  2. The distance `D_scene` to any stationary planet or target changes at a
     rate of `d(D_scene)/dt = V_scene` (when flying directly toward or away from
     the target).
  3. Because radar and target distances are converted to AU via:
       Distance (AU) = D_scene / 235
     the rate of change of distance on the radar is exactly:
       Radar Delta-d / Delta-t (AU/s) = V_scene / 235
  4. And the ship's velocity in kilometers per second is computed directly from
     the exact same vector:
       Velocity (km/s) = V_scene * (149,597,870.7 / 235)
                       = V_scene * 636,586.6838 km/s
  5. Both scientific notation (`e.g., 5.41 x 10^7 km/s`) and full comma-separated
     integer notation (`e.g., 54,109,868 km/s`) are shown simultaneously in the
     Flight Telemetry panel so every observer can verify that the speed matches
     the radar countdown second-by-second.

3.4 CANONICAL STAR TREK WARP FACTOR EQUATIONS (TNG OKUDA & TOS SCALES)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
The simulation implements the official Star Trek warp velocity equations in
`getStarTrekCanonicalWarpSpeed(warpFactor)`:
  1. Star Trek: The Next Generation (Okuda Scale, Warp 1 to 9):
       v(w) = w^(10/3) * c
     - Warp 1.0 = 1.00 c       = 2.998 x 10^5 km/s (299,792 km/s)
     - Warp 2.0 = 10.08 c      = 3.02 x 10^6 km/s
     - Warp 3.0 = 38.94 c      = 1.17 x 10^7 km/s
     - Warp 4.0 = 101.59 c     = 3.05 x 10^7 km/s
     - Warp 5.0 = 213.75 c     = 6.41 x 10^7 km/s
     - Warp 6.0 = 392.50 c     = 1.18 x 10^8 km/s
     - Warp 7.0 = 656.14 c     = 1.97 x 10^8 km/s
     - Warp 8.0 = 1,024.00 c   = 3.07 x 10^8 km/s
     - Warp 9.0 = 1,516.38 c   = 4.55 x 10^8 km/s
  2. Above Warp 9.0 (Asymptotic Exponential Approach toward Warp 10 Infinite
     Threshold):
     - Warp 9.2 ≈ 1,649 c
     - Warp 9.6 ≈ 1,909 c
     - Warp 9.9 ≈ 3,053 c      = 9.15 x 10^8 km/s
  3. Star Trek: The Original Series (TOS Cubic Scale Reference):
       v_TOS(w) = w^3 * c

3.5 COLLISION AVOIDANCE & SAFE STANDOFF VECTOR MATHEMATICS
----------------------------------------------------------
When plotting an automatic warp course to any planet, star, or space station,
`calculateSafeCoordinates()` in `/src/three/solarSystems.ts` prevents the ship
from flying inside solid planetary geometry:
  * It computes the Euclidean distance from the target coordinate to every
    celestial body's center and compares it against:
      SafeRadius = max(PlanetRadius * 2.5 + 140, RingOuterRadius + 140, 220)
  * If the destination lies inside `SafeRadius`, it projects a normalized
    approach vector `(ShipPos - BodyCenter) / ||ShipPos - BodyCenter||` and
    places the warp dropout waypoint cleanly in orbital standoff outside the
    planet's rings and atmosphere.

Q1: What is the main objective of this simulation?
A1: It is an interactive 3D spaceflight and astrophysics simulator modeled on the
    USS Enterprise (NCC-1701) from Star Trek. It lets users pilot a starship
    across 30 star systems, command the 3D bridge from 5 crew stations, defend
    against hostile fleets, and perform real-world space science missions
    involving spectroscopy, planetary magnetic fields, and radio astronomy.

Q2: Why does the ship start at 0% speed (Full Stop) when the app loads?
A2: Starting at Full Stop (`0 km/s`) gives the presenter or new visitor time to
    orient themselves, inspect the Earth and Moon in the Sol System, or open the
    3D Bridge and Star Map before engaging Impulse or Warp engines.

Q3: How do I move the ship forward and steer it?
A3: Increase the Impulse Throttle slider at the bottom (or click `1/4`, `1/2`,
    `FULL`), or press `Ctrl + Alt + 1..9` to engage Warp Drive. Steer the nose
    using `W/S` (or Up/Down arrows) for pitch, `A/D` (or Left/Right arrows) for
    yaw, and `Q/E` to roll.

Q4: Why did my manual steering (W, A, S, D) lock when I plotted a course on the map?
A4: When an automated Warp Course Plot is active, the ship's navigational
    computer locks the helm onto the destination vector. You can immediately
    retake manual control at any time by pressing `Ctrl + Alt + R` (or clicking
    `DROP WARP`).

Q5: How can I see the enemy ship when I am inside the 3D Bridge at the Tactical Station?
A5: When you switch to the Tactical Station (Key `3` in Bridge View), the lower
    control panel features a dedicated **Live Target Pinpoint Monitor** that
    mirrors the forward viewscreen, highlights the locked hostile ship, shows
    its shield and hull percentages, and lets you cycle targets (`<< PREV` /
    `NEXT >>`) and launch Photon Torpedoes (`T`) directly.

Q6: Can I zoom in and out both outside the ship and inside the 3D Bridge?
A6: Yes! You can use the Mouse Scroll Wheel, press the `+` and `-` keys, or click
    the `- / 100% / +` zoom buttons on the HUD in both External Chase/Orbit views
    and inside the 3D Bridge Interior. In Bridge View, you can also zoom the
    panoramic Main Viewscreen up to `8.0x` optical magnification.

Q7: What happens if my ship takes heavy damage in combat?
A7: You can press `U` when near any Starfleet Spacedock (or click the "Nearest
    Space Dock" button on the HUD to auto-warp to one). Docking clamps will
    engage, restoring shields and hull to 100%, reloading all 32 photon
    torpedoes, and allowing you to deploy 3D flying repair drones.

Q8: What happens if I fly into the Black Hole beyond Pluto?
A8: Located 21 AU past Pluto in the Sol System is the Sol Kerr Singularity. If
    you fly across its event horizon, gravitational lensing envelops the ship
    and transports you through an Einstein-Rosen bridge (wormhole), ejecting you
    from the incandescent White Hole in the Qo'noS (Klingon) system!

Q9: What is the 6-digit code in the top bar for?
A9: That is the **Science Exhibition Officer Profile System**. Visitors can
    create a 6-digit PIN code that saves their Starfleet Rank, XP, and mission
    grades so they can return to the booth later and resume their progress.

Q10: Where do the planetary surface textures for Earth, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn come from?
A10: The Sol System planets use real 2K equirectangular NASA/astrometric surface
     maps (stored locally in `/public/assets/planets/`) combined with procedural
     bump mapping, atmospheric glow shaders, and high-resolution concentric ring
     textures that accurately depict Saturn's Cassini Division and Encke Gap.

8.3 SECTION B: PHYSICS, ASTRONOMY & SPACE SCIENCE QUESTIONS (SCIENCE JUDGES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q11: What is the difference between "8.5c" and "8.5 Light-Years", and how does
     your simulation handle them?
A11: They measure two completely different physical dimensions:
     - `c` is a unit of **speed** (the speed of light in vacuum,
       `1c = 299,792.458 km/s`). So `8.5c` is a speed of `2,548,236 km/s`.
     - A `Light-Year (LY)` is a unit of **distance** (the distance light travels
       in one Julian year, `1 LY ≈ 9.461 x 10^12 km ≈ 63,241 AU`).
     Our simulation strictly expresses velocity in `km/s`, `AU/s`, and `c` in the
     Flight Telemetry menu, while expressing radar and target distances in
     Astronomical Units (`AU`).

Q12: How did you ensure that the ship's displayed speed (`km/s`) and the change
     in distance on the radar (`AU`) are mathematically consistent?
A12: Both values are derived from the exact same 3D velocity vector `v_scene`
      every frame:
     - We define `1 AU = 149,597,870.7 km = 235 scene units`
       (`1 scene unit = 636,586.6838 km`).
     - Radar distance to a target is `D_AU = D_scene / 235`, so the radar
       distance changes at `Delta-d / Delta-t = ||v_scene|| / 235 AU/s`.
     - The displayed velocity in `km/s` is `||v_scene|| * 636,586.6838 km/s`.
     Because both use the identical conversion factor, if the telemetry says the
     ship is moving at `1.00 AU/s` (`1.496 x 10^8 km/s`), the target distance on
     the radar decreases by exactly `1.00 AU` every second (`v = Delta-d / Delta-t`).

Q13: What mathematical formula does Star Trek use for Warp Factor speeds?
A13: In *Star Trek: The Next Generation* (the Michael Okuda scale), warp speed
     from Warp 1 to Warp 9 follows the power law:
       `v = w^(10/3) * c`
     where `w` is the Warp Factor and `c` is the speed of light (`299,792.458 km/s`).
     For example, Warp 1 is `1c`, Warp 4 is `4^(10/3)c ≈ 101.6c`, and Warp 9 is
     `9^(10/3)c ≈ 1,516.4c`. Above Warp 9, the exponent rises asymptotically
     toward infinity at Warp 10 (Eugene's Limit), reaching `3,053c` at Warp 9.9.
     In *The Original Series* (TOS), the simpler cubic formula `v = w^3 * c` was
     used. Both are computed in `/src/utils/scaleFormatters.ts`.

Q14: Is Faster-Than-Light (FTL) Warp Drive theoretically possible in real physics?
A14: Special Relativity forbids any object from locally accelerating through
     space faster than `c`. However, in 1994, physicist Miguel Alcubierre
     published the **Alcubierre Warp Metric** in General Relativity. Rather than
     moving through flat space faster than light, a warp drive contracts
     spacetime in front of the vessel and expands spacetime behind it while the
     ship sits inside a flat-spacetime "warp bubble" experiencing zero proper
     acceleration (no relativistic time dilation or G-force crushing).

Q15: What is the Rydberg Formula used in your Anomaly Investigation missions?
A15: The Rydberg formula predicts the exact wavelength `λ` of photons emitted or
     absorbed when an electron jumps between principal quantum energy levels
     `n_1` and `n_2` in a hydrogen atom:
       `1 / λ = R_H * (1 / n_1^2 - 1 / n_2^2)`
     where `R_H ≈ 1.097 x 10^7 m^-1`. For the transition from `n = 3` to `n = 2`
     (the Balmer H-Alpha line), this yields `λ = 656.28 nm`, which gives emission
     nebulae like the Orion Nebula their signature ruby-red glow.

Q16: Why are Forbidden Oxygen `[O III]` lines (500.7 nm) seen in nebulae but not
     in Earth laboratories?
A16: The `[O III]` transition at `500.7 nm` (teal-green) comes from a metastable
     quantum state that takes many seconds to decay spontaneously. In even the
     best vacuum chambers on Earth, atoms collide with chamber walls or other
     gas molecules long before they can radiate a photon. Only in the ultra-low
     density of interstellar nebulae (a few hundred atoms per cubic centimeter)
     can the ion remain undisturbed long enough to emit the `500.7 nm` photon.

Q17: How does the Planetary Magnetosphere Rescue mission teach real plasma physics?
A17: It models three real astrophysical processes:
     1. **Lorentz Force Deflection** (`F = q(E + v x B)`), which deflects
        charged solar wind protons around a planet's magnetic dipole.
     2. **Magnetopause Pressure Balance** (`P_ram = ρ * v^2 = B^2 / 2μ_0`), where
        a Coronal Mass Ejection compresses the magnetosphere.
     3. **Cyclotron Resonance** (`ω_c = |q|B / m`), where ions gyrate around
        magnetic field lines at a specific frequency. By tuning the Enterprise's
        deflector beam to the cyclotron frequency, escaping atmospheric ions are
        trapped in closed magnetic loops.

Q18: Why did you choose `1420.4 MHz` and `1612.2 MHz` for the First Contact missions?
A18: In real SETI (Search for Extraterrestrial Intelligence) radio astronomy:
     - `1420.405 MHz` is the **21-centimeter hyperfine spin-flip transition line
       of neutral atomic hydrogen (H I)**, the most abundant element in the
       universe.
     - `1612.231 MHz` is one of the primary microwave emission lines of the
       **hydroxyl radical (OH)**.
     The quiet microwave band between H (`1420 MHz`) and OH (`1666 MHz`) is
     famously called **"The Cosmic Water Hole"** (`H + OH = H2O`) by NASA SETI
     astronomers because any water-based civilization studying the galaxy would
     naturally monitor those exact frequencies!

Q19: What is Gravitational Redshift near the Quantum Singularity / Black Hole?
A19: According to Einstein's General Relativity, a black hole's event horizon
     occurs at the Schwarzschild radius `R_s = 2GM / c^2`. As photons climb out
     of the steep gravitational potential well near `R_s`, they lose energy
     (`E = hf`), shifting their wavelength toward the red end of the spectrum by
     `z_grav = (1 - R_s / r)^(-1/2) - 1`.

Q20: Why did you place the Sol Singularity 21 AU beyond Pluto?
A20: Pluto orbits at roughly `39.5 AU` from the Sun. By placing the Kerr
     Singularity at `~60.5 AU` (`21 AU` outside Pluto's orbit), it sits in the
     outer Kuiper Belt where visitors can intentionally warp to it to test
     gravitational lensing and wormhole transit without its event horizon
     interfering with navigation among the inner and outer planets.

8.4 SECTION C: PROGRAMMING, 3D GRAPHICS & ARCHITECTURE QUESTIONS (TECH JUDGES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q21: Which programming languages and frameworks did you use to build this?
A21: The application is built with **TypeScript (ESNext)**, **React 19** for the
     declarative LCARS HUD and scientific modals, **Three.js (WebGL)** for real-
     time 3D rendering, **GLSL** for custom vertex/fragment shaders, **Tailwind
     CSS 4** for styling, **Web Audio API / SAM-JS** for procedural sound and
     computer voice synthesis, and **Vite 8** as the build tool.

Q22: How does the 3D Bridge Interior run at 60 FPS without downloading massive
     3D model files (`.gltf` / `.fbx`)?
A22: Every console, chair, railing, turbolift alcove, and crew member in
     `/src/three/bridgeInterior.ts` is generated **procedurally in memory** using
     mathematical Three.js primitives (`CylinderGeometry`, `RingGeometry`,
     `BoxGeometry`, `SphereGeometry`, `CapsuleGeometry`) and PBR
     `MeshStandardMaterial` shaders. This eliminates network download bottlenecks,
     prevents missing-asset errors, and keeps GPU draw calls compact.

Q23: How are the live animated screens above the 3D Bridge consoles created?
A23: Each upper monitor uses an offscreen HTML5 `<canvas>` element bound to a
     `THREE.CanvasTexture`. Inside the animation loop, 2D canvas drawing routines
     render moving sine-wave spectroscopes, shield harmonic bar graphs, and
     astrometric radar sweeps, and set `texture.needsUpdate = true` so the 3D
     monitors glow and animate dynamically inside the WebGL scene.

Q24: How does the smooth camera transition between Bridge Stations work?
A24: When the user presses keys `1` through `5` (or clicks a crew mannequin or
     station button), the engine sets a target 3D camera position vector and
     target look-at quaternion for that station's eye point. On every frame, the
     camera smoothly interpolates its position (`Vector3.lerp`) and orientation
     (`Quaternion.slerp`) toward the target seat, and automatically hides that
     specific seat's 3D mannequin (`visible = false`) so the character mesh never
     blocks the first-person camera.

Q25: How did you fix the mouse-drag camera orientation so dragging right moves
     the view right and dragging up tilts up?
A25: In `SimulationEngine`, mouse delta movements (`deltaX`, `deltaY`) during
     pointer drag are mapped with natural signs to the camera yaw (`spherical.theta`
     / `bridgeLookYaw`) and pitch (`spherical.phi` / `bridgeLookPitch`), with
     clamping on polar angles (`phi`) to prevent gimbal lock or upside-down
     camera flipping.

Q26: How do you prevent React re-renders from slowing down the 60 FPS Three.js
     WebGL loop?
A26: The Three.js `SimulationEngine` runs completely outside React's render cycle
     via `requestAnimationFrame` inside a `useRef` container (`containerRef`).
     State updates back to React (`setShipState`) are throttled, and expensive
     calculations (like sorting universal celestial body distances) run on a
     600ms timer (`setInterval`) or inside memoized hooks (`useMemo`).

Q27: How are planetary textures and bump maps generated without freezing the browser?
A27: In `/src/three/solarSystems.ts`, we use four global `Map` caches
     (`textureCache`, `bumpTextureCache`, `starTextureCache`, `ringTextureCache`).
     Each texture is generated once—or asynchronously upgraded when a 2K NASA
     image finishes loading via `loadCachedImage()`—and reused across all render
     frames.

Q28: How does the 3D mouse-picking (clicking on a planet, starbase, or bridge
     station) work?
A28: We use `THREE.Raycaster`. When the user clicks the canvas, normalized device
     coordinates (`x` and `y` in `[-1, +1]`) are calculated from `clientX` and
     `clientY`. `raycaster.setFromCamera(mouse, camera)` casts a 3D ray into the
     scene and intersects either the bridge station hit-boxes (when in Bridge
     View) or planetary/enemy bounding spheres (when in External View).

Q29: How are the Phaser Beams and Photon Torpedoes rendered in 3D?
A29: - **Phasers**: Dual `THREE.CylinderGeometry` beams dynamically oriented and
       scaled each frame between the Enterprise's dorsal/ventral saucer phaser
       arrays and the locked target's 3D coordinates, paired with additive-blended
       impact glow sprites.
     - **Photon Torpedoes**: High-velocity projectile nodes wrapped in glowing
       crimson-amber `THREE.Sprite` corona halos that home in on the locked
       target vector and trigger a multi-particle `ParticleExplosion` upon impact.

Q30: How does the Adaptive AI Difficulty system work during combat waves?
A30: The engine tracks player combat metrics (hit accuracy, damage taken per
     wave, time to clear hostiles, and player level). Based on a rolling
     `combatRating` (centered at `100`), it dynamically scales enemy shield
     strength, weapon fire rate, and fleet composition (`Standard`, `Elevated`,
     `Severe`, `Critical`, `Legendary`).

Q31: How are the sound effects (phasers, torpedoes, warp hum, Red Alert) generated?
A31: They are synthesized in real time using the **Web Audio API**
     (`AudioContext`). For example, phasers combine frequency-modulated sawtooth
     oscillators passed through a bandpass filter, while photon torpedoes sweep a
     high-to-low exponential frequency envelope with white-noise bursts.

Q32: How does the USS Enterprise Computer Voice work offline or across different
     browsers?
A32: `computerVoice.ts` implements a dual-engine architecture: it supports the
     retro-futuristic **SAM (Software Automatic Mouth)** formant synthesizer
     (`sam-js`) as well as the browser's native `window.speechSynthesis` API,
     with customizable pitch, speed, throat, and mouth formant sliders in the
     Voice Settings modal.

Q33: How does the 6-Digit Officer Profile persist data without requiring a cloud
     login at the exhibition booth?
A33: `officerProfileStore` serializes the officer's 6-digit PIN record (Rank, XP,
     Level, Combat Rating, and Mission Transcripts) into browser `localStorage`.
     This guarantees instant, zero-latency profile switching even if the
     exhibition hall has unstable internet connectivity.

Q34: What happens if a player plots a course directly into the center of a star
     or planet?
A34: Our collision-avoidance algorithm `calculateSafeCoordinates()` checks the
     destination `[x, y, z]` against the physical radius and ring radius of every
     celestial body in the galaxy. If the point lies inside the safety envelope,
     it automatically offsets the warp dropout point along the ship's approach
     vector to a safe orbital standoff distance.

Q35: How can this project be extended in the future?
A35: Future expansions could include WebXR (VR headset support for walking around
     the 3D Enterprise Bridge), multi-player cooperative bridge crews via
     WebSockets (where 5 students on separate tablets control Captain, Helm,
     Tactical, Science, and Engineering simultaneously), and relativistic Doppler
     starfield color-shifting at high warp factors.
